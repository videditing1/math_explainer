How many people do you need in a room before two of them probably share a birthday? Gut instinct says around one hundred eighty. The real answer is just twenty-three. Here's the trick: you're not comparing birthdays with yours. You're comparing every pair. Twenty-three people make two hundred fifty-three pairs, each one a small chance at a match. Multiply the odds that every pair misses, and it drops below one half. By seventy people, a match is a ninety-nine point nine percent certainty.
